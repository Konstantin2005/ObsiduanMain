# Anchoring Bias

## Overview
The tendency to rely too heavily on the first piece of information encountered (the "anchor") when making decisions.

## Detailed Content
Anchoring bias occurs when initial information, even if irrelevant, disproportionately influences subsequent judgments and estimates. This bias is particularly strong in numerical estimation tasks.

### Mechanisms
- **Initial value effect**: First number sets reference point
- **Adjustment insufficiency**: People don't adjust enough from the anchor
- **Context dependency**: Anchor effectiveness depends on context

### Real-World Impact
- Salary negotiations
- Price negotiations
- Legal judgments
- Business forecasting

### Psychological Basis
Anchors create mental reference points that serve as starting points for cognitive adjustments, but these adjustments are often insufficient.
