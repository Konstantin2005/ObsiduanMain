# Framing Effect

## Overview
The tendency to react differently to the same information depending on how it's presented or framed.

## Detailed Content
The framing effect demonstrates how the presentation of information influences decision-making, even when the objective content remains identical. This bias affects risk perception and choice behavior.

### Mechanisms
- **Gain vs. loss framing**: People prefer gains framed positively, losses framed negatively
- **Risk aversion**: Different risk preferences based on framing
- **Context dependence**: Same information has different impacts based on presentation

### Real-World Impact
- Marketing and advertising
- Medical decision-making
- Financial planning
- Legal negotiations

### Psychological Basis
Framing effects reveal the dual-process nature of decision-making, where emotional and rational systems respond differently to framed information.

## Examples
- Choosing surgery with 90% survival rate vs. 10% mortality rate
- Investment options presented as gains vs. losses
- Health messages framed as warnings vs. benefits

## Impact
Distorts decision-making, creates inconsistent choices, and undermines rational evaluation.
