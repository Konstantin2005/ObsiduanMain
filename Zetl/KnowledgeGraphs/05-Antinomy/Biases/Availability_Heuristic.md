# Availability Heuristic

## Overview
The tendency to overestimate the likelihood of events based on their availability in memory, particularly those that are more vivid, recent, or emotionally charged.

## Detailed Content
The availability heuristic is a mental shortcut that relies on immediate examples that come to mind when evaluating a specific topic. This bias affects risk assessment, decision-making, and probability judgments.

### Mechanisms
- **Recency effect**: Recent events are weighted more heavily
- **Salience effect**: Dramatic or emotional events are more available
- **Media influence**: News coverage skews perception of frequency

### Real-World Impact
- Overestimating crime rates after media coverage
- Misjudging flight safety after plane crashes
- Poor investment decisions based on recent market performance

### Cognitive Load
This heuristic reduces cognitive effort by using easily recalled examples rather than conducting systematic analysis.
