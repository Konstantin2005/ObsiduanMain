# Sunk Cost Fallacy

## Overview
The tendency to continue investing in a failing course of action because of previously invested resources (time, money, effort).

## Detailed Content
The sunk cost fallacy causes people to make irrational decisions by allowing past investments to influence future choices, even when continuing the investment is no longer rational or beneficial.

### Mechanisms
- **Past investment bias**: Previous costs influence current decisions
- **Escalation of commitment**: Increasing investment in failing endeavors
- **Loss aversion**: Fear of admitting failure outweighs rational analysis

### Real-World Impact
- Business project continuation
- Relationship persistence
- Investment decisions
- Personal goal pursuit

### Psychological Basis
The fallacy combines loss aversion with commitment bias, creating powerful inertia against change even when change is beneficial.

## Examples
- Continuing to fund a failing business project
- Staying in an unhappy relationship "for the sake of invested time"
- Buying more lottery tickets after losses

## Impact
Wastes resources, prevents learning, and creates cycles of poor decision-making.
