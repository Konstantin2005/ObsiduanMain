# Dunning-Kruger Effect

## Overview
The cognitive bias in which people with low ability at a task overestimate their ability, while those with high ability underestimate their ability.

## Detailed Content
The Dunning-Kruger effect reveals a metacognitive bias where individuals lack the expertise to recognize their own incompetence. This creates a double burden: poor performance and lack of awareness of that poor performance.

### Mechanisms
- **Metacognitive deficiency**: Inability to distinguish between knowledge and ignorance
- **Confidence calibration**: Poor performers overestimate their abilities
- **Skill-blindness**: Highly skilled individuals underestimate their competence

### Real-World Impact
- Workplace performance evaluations
- Academic assessments
- Safety-critical decisions
- Learning and skill development

### Psychological Basis
The effect stems from the fact that competence requires not just knowledge, but also the ability to recognize what one doesn't know.

## Examples

- Poor students overestimating their academic performance
- New employees overestimating their capabilities

## Impact
Impedes learning, creates safety risks, and undermines accurate self-assessment.
