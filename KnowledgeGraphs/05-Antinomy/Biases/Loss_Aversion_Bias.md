# Loss Aversion Bias

## Overview
The tendency to prefer avoiding losses to acquiring equivalent gains, causing asymmetric decision-making around risk.

## Detailed Content
Loss aversion bias is a cognitive bias where people experience the pain of losses more strongly than the pleasure of equivalent gains. This affects risk assessment and decision-making across various domains.

### Mechanisms
- **Asymmetric response**: Losses hurt more than gains feel good
- **Risk aversion**: Preferring sure gains over probabilistic losses
- **Status quo preference**: Avoiding changes that might lead to loss

### Real-World Impact
- Investment decisions
- Insurance purchases
- Career changes
- Health behaviors

### Psychological Basis
Loss aversion is thought to be an evolutionary adaptation, where avoiding losses was more critical for survival than acquiring gains.

- Avoiding beneficial risks
- Staying in unfulfilling jobs
- Skipping preventive health measures

## Impact
Reduces risk-taking, limits opportunities, and creates suboptimal resource allocation.
